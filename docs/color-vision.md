# Color vision deficiency: details

Generated by `python3 scripts/accessibility.py --write`; do not edit by hand.

Colors are simulated with the Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes (2009) matrices at full severity and compared with CIE76 ΔE (about 2.3 is the smallest difference a person with normal vision notices; above ~15 two colors are clearly different). Each theme is rated per type of color blindness on three things, and the rating is the worst of the three:

| What | ✅ recommended | ⚠️ use with care | ❌ not recommended |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Closest pair of terminal text colors (ANSI 1-6, 9-14) | ΔE ≥ 8 | ≥ 4 | below |
| Red vs green, normal and bright (success/error, diffs) | ΔE ≥ 15 | ≥ 8 | below |
| Closest pair of the 9 syntax colors | ΔE ≥ 5 | ≥ 2.5 | below |

❌ does not mean unusable: it means that some colors that carry meaning become (nearly) indistinguishable, so the theme should not be the only thing telling the two apart. Text contrast does not depend on color vision and is enforced for every theme (4.5:1 syntax, 4:1 comments, 7:1 in the high contrast themes).

**Achromatopsia (no color at all)**: no theme separates its colors by lightness alone, so none is rated. Readability is still guaranteed by the contrast contract; the two high contrast themes (NERV High Contrast, EVA Mark.06 High Contrast) give the strongest text contrast, and bold/italic/position carry the rest.
